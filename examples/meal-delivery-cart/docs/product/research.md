# Market watch: meal-delivery cart

Idea (from the human): "A meal-delivery cart where customers add dishes, apply promotions and see the delivery fees before they order."

Everything below is a hypothesis, not a verdict. Web search was available; only search-result excerpts were read, no page was opened in full. A claim with no source, or whose source is weak, is marked *unverified*. No market size is given: none was found.

## 1. The problem and who has it

- **Who.** Customers ordering prepared meals for delivery from restaurants or kitchens, and the operators who sell to them. *Unverified*: no interview or study was found for this project; the personas are assumed.
- **The situation.** The customer builds a basket, then meets a stack of lines: food subtotal, delivery fee, service fee, possible small-order fee, tax, tip. A promotion may remove one line but, per one secondary source, may change another (see Sources, S1). The customer cannot easily tell what the order will cost until late in checkout. *Unverified* as a pain felt by customers: the sources only describe the fee structure.
- **How they cope today.** They read the checkout summary, compare across apps, or order by phone or in person. A substitute habit: ordering directly from a restaurant's own page or by phone, where the price is whatever the restaurant says. *Unverified*.

| Player | Does well | Fails at / limit | Fees as published | Source |

## 3. Blue or red ocean

Factors buyers weigh (assumed, *unverified*): price of the food, total fee transparency before ordering, promotion clarity, delivery speed, restaurant choice, trust in the final price.

| Factor | Marketplace apps | Own page / phone | Gap? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Restaurant choice | Strong | Weak | no |
| Delivery speed | Strong | Varies | no |
| Fee visibility before ordering | Shown at checkout (S2) | Not applicable | Possibly early in the flow, not only at checkout: *unverified* |
| Promotion clarity (what a code does to every line, fees included) | Weak per one secondary source (S1) | Rare | Possible |
| Final-price trust | Fee stack split in several lines | Simple | Possible |

Reading: choice and speed look like a **red ocean**, crowded and competed on the same factors. A **blue gap may exist** on one factor: a total price, fees and promotion effect shown as the customer builds the cart, with every line explained. What would contradict it: marketplaces already show a live total in the cart (not verified here; the checkout was the only place found), or customers do not care about seeing fees early.

## 4. Trends and constraints

- **Fee transparency regulation (US).** The federal rule finalized in December 2024 requires mandatory fees to be disclosed in the advertised price, but only for live-event tickets and short-term lodging; it does not cap prices and allows itemizing (S3). It does not cover food delivery as reported; its current legal status is *unverified*.
- **Fee caps on delivery platforms.** New York City capped delivery commissions at 15% and other services at 5% (3% for transaction fees) and requires platform licences; the platforms sued (S4). Outcome of the suit is *unverified*. Rules differ by city and country, so any real launch needs a legal check per market.
- **Fee structures are moving.** DoorDash announced a new structure in some areas (S2): a product built on fixed fee lines can age quickly.
- **Money handling.** Tax, tip and rounding rules differ by place; none was researched. *Unverified*.

## 5. What is unknown

These change the conclusion if false; test them first.

1. Customers want to see fees and promotion effect *before* the checkout step, and this changes whether they order.
2. Existing carts do not already show a live total with fees. Needs a manual check of real apps.
3. A promotion can change fees (S1 only, weak source); if it is rare, the promotion-clarity gap is thin.
4. Who the buyer is: the customer, or the operator who wants the cart on their own site (own page substitute).
5. Which market and currency apply; the fee and tax rules depend on it.

# Phase 1: market watch

## Goal

Know where the product can stand out before choosing what it is. The output is `docs/product/research.md`: sourced hypotheses, not a verdict.

## What to cover

1. **The problem and who has it.** The people, the situation, how they cope today. One paragraph each, no solution yet.
2. **Competitors and substitutes.** Direct competitors, indirect ones, and the habit that serves as a substitute (a spreadsheet, a manual process). For each: what it does well, what it fails at, price, and the source.
3. **Blue or red ocean.** A red ocean is a crowded market where competition is on the same factors. A blue ocean is a space where the product competes on other factors, or serves people nobody serves. Compare the competitors on 4 to 6 factors the buyers weigh; where every competitor scores the same, the market is red; where a factor is absent or neglected, a blue gap may exist. State which it looks like, and what would contradict it.
4. **Trends and constraints.** Regulation, technology, behavior shifts that open or close a gap.
5. **What is unknown.** The assumptions that, if false, change the conclusion. These are the first things to test.

## Rules

- Every claim cites its source: a page, a report, an interview. No source: write *unverified*.
- No invented figure. A missing market size stays missing.
- Without web access, say so, ask the user for the competitors and sources they know, and work from those.
- End on a multiple-choice question: which positioning to carry into the vision (2 to 4 options, with a recommendation).
